# High-Level Design (HLD) – C++

A structured repository for learning and practicing **High-Level System Design**, with a focus on scalable, reliable, and high-performance systems.

The goal is to strengthen my understanding of system architecture, distributed systems, and engineering trade-offs, particularly for **C++ software engineering and system design interviews**.

## Objectives

- Understand the fundamentals of high-level system design.
- Design systems that can scale to millions of users and devices.
- Analyze performance, latency, throughput, availability, and reliability.
- Explore architectural trade-offs and justify design decisions.
- Apply system design concepts to real-world applications, including embedded systems, camera surveillance, and distributed services.

## Topics Covered

| Section | Topic | Key Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Requirements & Capacity Estimation | Functional/non-functional requirements, RPS, throughput, storage, peak traffic |

| 1.8 | Reliability & Failure Handling | Retries, timeouts, circuit breakers, graceful degradation |

## My Learning Approach

For each topic, I focus on:

1. **Concepts** – Understanding how the technology works.
2. **Design Decisions** – Identifying when and why to use a particular approach.
3. **Trade-offs** – Evaluating scalability, latency, consistency, cost, and complexity.
4. **Practical Examples** – Connecting concepts to real-world engineering problems.
5. **Interview Practice** – Solving scenario-based system design questions.

## Real-World Applications

I aim to apply these concepts to systems such as:

- **Camera Surveillance Systems** – Real-time video processing, bounded queues, and low-latency communication.
- **Distributed Device Monitoring** – Device heartbeats, telemetry, communication, and fault recovery.
- **Task Scheduling Systems** – Queues, worker pools, concurrency, and scheduling.
- **Scalable Backend Services** – Load balancing, caching, databases, and asynchronous processing.
